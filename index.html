Xana operating system · emergency preparedness & response

Protect people. Then everything else.

Practical training for retail/wholesale, cold storage, deli, liquor and pharmacy. The same first ten minutes at every Xana location, and what to protect once the people are safe.

Branch staff session · September 2026 · retail / wholesale · cold storage · deli · liquor · pharmacy

01 · Start here

What you should be able to do after this session.

  • Recognize. Know what counts as an emergency or incident in the Xana environment.
  • Respond. Execute the FIRST 10 MINUTES without improvising.
  • Control. Know when to stop an area, isolate a hazard, or place product on HOLD.
  • Escalate. Know who takes the lead and when to call specialist / external support.
  • Recover. Re-open only when safety, product and technical checks are complete.

The procedure is designed to make emergency response consistent across every Xana location, so a customer, a product or a colleague is handled the same way at every branch.

03 · The operating logic

Clarity beats heroics. Follow the sequence.

The response sequence is simple. In an emergency, it is the one thing nobody has to invent.

  1. Protect people. Life safety comes first.
  2. Control the hazard. Stop or isolate what is unsafe.
  3. Protect product. Hold, segregate and trace affected stock.
  4. Communicate. Call the right people; keep instructions clear.
  5. Recover. Repair, assess, authorize, restart.
  6. Learn. Record, investigate, correct, prevent recurrence.

Key rule: do not keep trading an unsafe activity to protect sales.

05 · Incident command

One clear lead. Specialist support around the lead.

The Branch / Outlet Lead is the Incident Lead until relieved.

  • Shift Lead / Supervisor. Customer direction, headcount, area control
  • Pharmacy Lead / Pharmacist. Patient safety, medicines, clinical escalation
  • Deli / Food Safety Lead. Food control, traceability, sanitation
  • Facilities / Maintenance. Utilities, refrigeration, technical recovery
  • Security. Access control, threat response, police coordination
  • Quality / Compliance. Product safety, investigation, disposition
  • Operations / COO. Resources, continuity, leadership escalation
  • Finance / Administration. Emergency procurement, records, claims

The first ten minutes.

06 · Drill this

The first ten minutes.

  1. Alert. Raise the alarm and notify the Branch Lead.
  2. Protect. Move people away from danger.
  3. Stop / isolate. Stop the unsafe operation or isolate the area.
  4. Evacuate / shelter. Use the site emergency plan.
  5. Account. Headcount, and report missing persons.
  6. Call. Emergency, security, maintenance or medical, as the situation requires.
  7. Protect product. HOLD / DO NOT SELL where required.
  8. Document. Start the incident record.
  9. Escalate. Operations plus the relevant function.
  10. Recover. Restart only after checks and authorization.

Steps 1-6 are never swapped. Steps 7-10 run in parallel with the response, not after it.

Response playbook · 07 · fire / smoke

Fire is a life-safety event first.

Product protection comes only after people are safe.

Do first

  1. Raise the alarm and move away from the source.
  2. Call the emergency / fire response number.
  3. Only a trained person may use a suitable extinguisher, and only if the fire is small and escape remains possible.
  4. Evacuate through marked exits; no personal belongings.
  5. Assemble, headcount, report missing persons.

Then

Quarantine exposed product: heat · smoke · soot · firefighting agents · water · structural contamination.

Area-specific

Deli

Exposed ready-to-eat food goes on HOLD.

Pharmacy

Protect medicines and patient confidentiality.

Liquor / Retail

Broken glass is cleaned only after life-safety is secured.

Response playbook · 08 · water / chemical / gas

Often localized. Isolate first, then decide the footprint.

These are often localized emergencies: isolate first, then decide the operational footprint.

Water / sewage

Keep people away from electrical hazards. Stop the affected operation and isolate the source if safe. Prevent traffic into clean food, pharmacy or stock areas. Segregate water- or sewage-exposed products. Get technical / regulatory clearance if needed.

Chemical spill

Keep people away; do not touch unidentified substances. Isolate and use the SDS and trained response support. Prevent contamination of food, medicines and packaging. Dispose and clean only through the approved process. Escalate if exposure or contamination occurs.

Gas leak

Do not create an ignition source. Isolate only if safe and within your authority. Evacuate the affected area as required. Call emergency / technical support. Do not resume until cleared.

Trainer cue: ask “What must NOT be done?” Unsafe improvisation is a recurring failure mode.

09 · Xana-critical · power outage & cold-chain failure

For Xana, a utility failure can become a product-integrity and patient-safety emergency.

Power outage

Protect people: lighting, access, movement. Keep cold-room, freezer and pharmacy refrigerator doors closed. Record the outage start time and monitor temperatures. Use approved backup power only. No improvising connections. Use the approved POS / dispensing downtime process. Escalate if safety, cold chain, medicine availability or trading continuity is threatened.

Cold-chain failure

Confirm the reading using the monitoring device. Identify affected products and the time window. Label HOLD / DO NOT SELL where required. Transfer critical products only under an approved, validated plan. Do not release until the responsible technical / quality / pharmacy decision-maker approves. Record temperature history, repairs and product disposition.

Mental model: cold chain is a product-integrity control, not just a refrigeration issue.

11 · Product control

HOLD means HOLD.

Questionable product is not “probably okay.” It is controlled until assessed.

Food / Deli

Heat · smoke · water · sewage · chemical · pest · bodily fluid · temperature abuse.

Cold-chain food / beverages

Temperature excursion · refrigeration failure · prolonged outage.

Pharmacy medicines

Temperature excursion · contamination · water / smoke / fire · storage failure · security concern.

Liquor / Retail stock

Damage · contamination · broken glass · water / fire / smoke · security loss.

Hold / do not sell Do not sell · Do not serve · Do not dispense · Do not move without control
12 · Other incidents

Not every incident is a fire, but every incident still needs command, control and escalation.

The rest of the risk picture.

  1. Medical injury. First aid within training, medical escalation, control the scene.
  2. Foodborne illness. Stop the affected process / product, isolate lots, preserve traceability, escalate.
  3. Biological / bodily fluid. Restrict the area, trained response and approved cleaning, quarantine the exposure.
  4. Pest infestation. Isolate affected stock or area, notify pest control and Quality.
  5. Security / violence / sabotage. Life safety, disengage, secure the area, security and police.
  6. POS / communication outage. Approved downtime process, protect records, reconcile on restoration.
13 · Recovery

When are we allowed to re-open?

“The hazard looks gone” is not the same as “the operation is safe to restart.”

1 · People safe

No continuing exposure or unsafe condition.

2 · Hazard controlled

Fire, water, gas, chemical, security or technical hazard is controlled.

3 · Product assessed

Affected product is released, held or disposed through the responsible function.

4 · Technical / required approval

Facilities / Quality / Pharmacy / the relevant authority confirms readiness where required.

Document the incident, the corrective actions and the re-opening authorization.

14 · Preparedness

A branch that is prepared responds faster.

Readiness is visible before the emergency happens. These are the twelve things a prepared branch can put its hand on.

  • Emergency contacts are current and posted
  • Exits and the assembly point are known and clear
  • Fire equipment and alarms are maintained
  • First-aid kit and trained first aiders are current
  • Cold-chain monitoring works
  • Backup power and contingency are understood
  • Chemicals are labelled, segregated and SDS are accessible
  • Gas safety is understood where applicable
  • HOLD / DO NOT SELL process is understood
  • POS / communication downtime process works
  • Training and drills are practiced
  • Incidents and tests are reviewed and actions closed

Target state: no tribal knowledge. The branch can show the control, the contact, the plan and the record.

15 · Exercise

Scenario 01: 6:45 PM power outage.

The situation

You are the Branch / Outlet Lead. The branch is busy. The cold room is full. Pharmacy is still serving patients. POS is down.

Power has failed. Customers are still inside. Lighting is poor. Refrigeration is silent. A refrigerator alarm in pharmacy has not yet been checked.

Time pressure

What do you do in the first 10 minutes?

  1. Who is Incident Lead?
  2. What happens to customer movement / lighting?
  3. What happens to cold room / freezer / pharmacy refrigerator doors?
  4. What gets put on HOLD?
  5. What is the approved response for POS downtime?
  6. When do you escalate to Operations / technical support?
→ reveal the answers
  1. The Branch / Outlet Lead, until relieved.
  2. Control customer movement, keep safe lighting, stop unsafe areas, account for everyone inside.
  3. Keep them closed. Record the outage start time and monitor temperatures.
  4. Any temperature-exposed cold-chain or pharmacy stock: HOLD / DO NOT SELL, segregated and traced.
  5. The approved POS / dispensing downtime process, protecting records and reconciling on restoration.
  6. Immediately if safety, cold chain, medicine availability or trading continuity is threatened, plus the relevant function.

The two most common failures here: opening the cold-room doors “to check”, and letting the queue keep the pharmacy counter running without confirming the refrigerator.

16 · Exercise

Scenario 02: Smoke in the deli.

The situation

A staff member smells burning near the deli. There is visible smoke. Customers are present near the food display.

Smoke is visible. There is ready-to-eat food in the service area, cooking equipment in the deli, and customers nearby.

Role play

What should the first observer do, and what should the Branch Lead do next?

→ reveal the answers
  1. Raise the alarm and move away from the source.
  2. Call the emergency / fire response number.
  3. Evacuate customers and staff through safe exits.
  4. Headcount and report missing persons.
  5. Keep the smoke-affected area closed.
  6. Quarantine exposed ready-to-eat food and food-contact surfaces pending assessment.

Only a trained person uses an extinguisher, and only if the fire is small and escape remains possible. Everything else waits.

17 · Knowledge check

Five questions before you leave.

Use a quick round-the-room response. No slides for the answers.

  1. Who is the Incident Lead at a branch?The Branch / Outlet Lead, until relieved.
  2. What is the first priority in any emergency?Protect people. Life safety comes first; product protection comes after.
  3. When do you use HOLD / DO NOT SELL?Whenever product safety is questionable: heat, smoke, water, chemical, temperature abuse, damage or security loss. It stays controlled until assessed.
  4. When can an affected area re-open?Only when people are safe, the hazard is controlled, the product has been assessed, and the required technical / quality / pharmacy approval is given.
  5. What is one emergency control you should be able to demonstrate today?Any one of: the assembly point, the emergency contacts, closing the cold-room doors, the HOLD process, or the POS downtime process.
→ reveal the answers
